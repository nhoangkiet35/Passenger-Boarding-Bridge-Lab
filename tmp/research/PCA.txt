DANGER: TO REDUCE THE RISK OF FIRE OR ELECTRIC SHOCK, FOLLOW 
THESE INSTRUCTIONS CAREFULLY 
 
This unit is only intended to be operated and maintained by competent persons having the 
necessary knowledge regarding delivery of pre-conditioned air to aircraft. Prior to use, 
service and maintenance, the operator must be familiar with all parts of this manual.  
 
This unit is not intended for use by persons (including children) with reduced physical, sensory or 
mental capabilities, or lack of experience and knowledge, unless they have been given supervision or 
instruction concerning use of the unit by a person responsible for their safety. 
 
This unit is for outdoor use and not accessible for general public. 
 
The appliance shall be installed in accordance with national wiring regulations. 
 
Electric shock 
• To ensure personal health and safety, the electrical installation must fulfil local 
regulations and legislation. 
• Touching live electrical parts can cause fatal shocks and severe burns. 
• Internal parts where the voltage exceeds 50 V are covered and/or marked with:  
• Keep all panels and covers securely in place. 
• Always switch off and lock the input breaker before accessing internal parts. 
• Only allow qualified people to remove covers for maintenance or troubleshooting. 
 
Moving/mechanical/hot parts 
• Keep away from fans. 
• Keep panels, covers and guards closed and securely in place. 
• Only allow qualified people to remove covers for maintenance or troubleshooting. 
• Keep hands, hair, loose clothing and tools away from moving parts. 
• Keep cooling modules securely in place during maintenance and repair.  
• Do not touch hot parts.  
• Allow for a cooling period before doing maintenance. 
 
Hose(s)  
• Always connect the hose(s) to the aircraft before starting the unit to avoid personal 
injury or damage to equipment that may be present on the apron. Loose hoses may 
constitute a risk if put under pressure when not connected.

2.2 Instructions for handling, installation, cleaning, servicing and 
disposal of A2L refrigerant 
 
• Auxiliary devices which may be a potential ignition source shall not be installed in the duct 
work. Examples of such potential ignition sources are hot surfaces with a temperature 
exceeding 700°C / 1290°F and electric switching devices. 
• For units using A2L refrigerant connected via an air duct system to one or more rooms, 
manufacturer or declared suitable with the refrigerant shall be installed in connecting ductwork. 
The manufacturer shall list in the instructions all approved auxiliary devices by manufacturer 
and model number for use with the specific unit, if those devices have a potential to become an 
ignition source. 
• If the unit using flammable refrigerant is installed in an unventilated area, the area shall be so 
constructed that should any refrigerant leak, it will not stagnate so as to create a fire or 
explosion hazard. 
• Continuous air circulation required for proper functioning. Unit must be powered except for 
service. 
• Work procedures to perform repairs, decommissioning, or maintenance shall be in accordance 
with local and national regulations and as per below requirements. 
• Work shall be undertaken under a controlled procedure so as to minimise the risk of a 
flammable gas or vapour being present while the work is being performed. 
• All maintenance staff and others working in the local area shall be instructed on the nature of 
work being carried out. Work in confined spaces shall be avoided. 
• The area shall be checked with an appropriate refrigerant detector prior to and during work, to 
ensure the technician is aware of potentially toxic or flammable atmospheres. 
• Ensure that the leak detection equipment being used is suitable for use with all applicable 
refrigerants, i. e. non-sparking, adequately sealed or intrinsically safe. 
• If any hot work is to be conducted on the refrigerating equipment or any associated parts, 
appropriate fire extinguishing equipment shall be available to hand. 
• Have a dry powder or CO2 fire extinguisher adjacent to the charging area. 
• No person carrying out work in relation to a REFRIGERATING SYSTEM which involves 
exposing any pipe work shall use any sources of ignition in such a manner that it may lead to 
the risk of fire or explosion. 
• All possible ignition sources, including cigarette smoking, should be kept sufficiently far away 
from the site of installation, repairing, removing and disposal, during which refrigerant can 
possibly be released to the surrounding space. 
• Prior to work taking place, the area around the equipment is to be surveyed to make sure that 
there are no flammable hazards or ignition risks. 
• No Smoking' signs shall be displayed. 
• Ensure that the area is in the open or that it is adequately ventilated before breaking into the 
system or conducting any hot work. 
• A degree of ventilation shall continue during the period that the work is carried out. 
• The ventilation should safely disperse any released refrigerant and preferably expel it externally 
into the atmosphere.

• Where electrical components are being changed, they shall be fit for the purpose and to the 
correct specification. 
 
• At all times the manufacturer’s maintenance and service guidelines shall be followed. If in 
doubt, consult the manufacturer’s technical department for assistance. 
• Repair and maintenance to electrical components shall include initial safety checks and 
component inspection procedures. 
• If a fault exists that could compromise safety, then no electrical supply shall be connected to 
the circuit until it is satisfactorily dealt with. If the fault cannot be corrected immediately but it is 
necessary to continue operation, an adequate temporary solution shall be used. This shall be 
reported to the owner of the equipment, so all parties are advised. 
• Initial safety checks shall include that capacitors are discharged. This shall be done in a safe 
manner to avoid possibility of sparking and that no live electrical components and wiring are 
exposed while charging, recovering, or purging to system. There should be continuity of earth 
bonding. 
• Sealed electrical components shall be replaced. 
• Intrinsically safe components must be replaced. 
• Check that cabling will not be subject to wear, corrosion, excessive pressure, vibration, sharp 
edges or any other adverse environmental effects. The check shall also take into account the 
effects of aging or continual vibration from sources such as compressors or fans. 
• Under no circumstances shall potential sources of ignition be used in the searching for or 
detection of refrigerant leaks. A halide torch (or any other detector using a naked flame) shall 
not be used. The following leak detection methods are deemed acceptable for all refrigerant 
systems: Electronic leak detectors may be used to detect refrigerant leaks but, in the case of 
FLAMMABLE REFRIGERANTS, the sensitivity may not be adequate, or may need re-
calibration. (Detection equipment shall be calibrated in a refrigerant-free area.) 
• Ensure that the detector is not a potential source of ignition and is suitable for the refrigerant 
used. 
• Leak detection equipment shall be set at a percentage of the LFL of the refrigerant and shall be 
calibrated to the refrigerant employed, and the appropriate percentage of gas (25 % maximum) 
is confirmed. 
• Leak detection fluids are also suitable for use with most refrigerants but the use of detergents 
containing chlorine shall be avoided as the chlorine may react with the refrigerant and corrode 
the copper pipe-work. 
• When breaking into the refrigerant circuit to make repairs – or for any other purpose – 
conventional procedures shall be used. However, for FLAMMABLE REFRIGERANTS it is 
important that best practice is followed since flammability is a consideration. 
• The following procedure shall be adhered to: 
   a) safely remove refrigerant following local and national regulations; 
   b) purge the circuit with inert gas; 
   c) evacuate (optional for A2L); 
   d) purge with inert gas (optional for A2L); 
   e) open the circuit by cutting or brazing 
• The refrigerant charge shall be recovered into the correct recovery cylinders if venting is not 
allowed by local and national codes.

3.0 Product Description 
The ITW GSE 3500 PCA is a completely self -contained all-electric bridge or ground -mounted unit for 
comfortable air conditioning of narrow-body, wide-body, jumbo and super jumbo aircraft cabins. The PCA 
suits all types of aircraft and all climates in countries with a mains voltage frequency of 50/60 Hz. 
 
The PCA is built in a modular design, which ensures easy maintenance and cleaning. The modular 
design also helps to keep the MTTR (Mean Time To Repair) to the very minimum. 
The compressors and main blower are powered by individual VFDs (Variable Frequency Drive s), all 
regulated and controlled by a central control module. This type of control for the two types of components 
is a unique feature of the ITW GSE 3500 PCA – ensuring that the PCA’s air and cooling provision 
matches the actual levels required. 
 
Due to the modular design , the range of trained staff 
required is kept to a minimum. For instance , it is not 
necessary to have maintenance staff capable of handling 
refrigerant on duty at all times, as it is possible to replace 
a Self-Contained Cooling Module instead. 
 
The cooling power required to cool an aircraft down to a 
comfortable temperature is dependent on three main 
conditions: 
 - The required aircraft cabin temperature 
 - The size/type of aircraft 
 - The ambient outside air temperature/humidity 
 
A ‘comfortable’ aircraft cabin temperature will typically be in the range of 24-28 °C.  
Unless otherwise specified, the climatic condition s relating to the outside ambient temperature and 
humidity are taken from the ASHRAE Handbook – Fundamentals, Chapter 14 (American Society of 
Heating, Refrigerating and Air -Conditioning Engineers). The figures used are based on the 0.4% 
percentile. The values given in the ASHRAE Handbook – Fundamentals are based on several years of 
data collected from various locations around the world, and are thus based on solid statistical material. 
 
This means that during a year (8760 hours) , for 99.6% (100 minus 0.4%) of the time on average, the 
ambient temperature will not exceed the value used for the calculation of the cooling capacity. It also 
means that for 99.6% of the time on average, the PCA system will be able to cool/maintain the aircraft 
cabin at the design temperature (or lower) and that for only 0.4% of the time (35 hours per year) the 
aircraft cabin temperature is likely to be slightly higher than the design temperature.

3.1 Mechanical design 
The ITW GSE 3500 PCA is constructed around an anti -corrosion treated, bolted steel frame, which 
supports the modular design, including the main blower, cooling modules, internal air ducts, electrical 
panel and magnetics. 
 
The main blower is a direct -driven centrifugal-type fan and can easily be accessed by removing a side 
cover. To avoid vibrations, the main blower is mounted on vibration dampers , with flexible connections 
between the main blower and the stainless steel air ducts. 
 
All cooling modules can be accessed by extracting their respective trays. By means of a forklift, a cooling 
module can easily be removed for maintenance, without the need for any on-site welding or other kinds 
of hot work. The extractable cooling modules also allow access to the internal stainless steel air ducts 
for inspection and cleaning. More information regarding cooling modules be found in section 3.3. 
 
The internal air duct (s) are made of stainless steel and supplied with  integrated drain tubing for 
connection with the membrane-type condensate pumps, which are located at the main blower. The air 
duct following the main blower is insulated with 10 mm thick closed-cell rubber foam insulation to reduce 
losses and avoid condensation. The same duct is equipped with a photoelectric smoke detector, enabling 
the PCA to be shut down before any smoke reaches the aircraft cabin. 
 
The electrical panel can be accessed from the opposite side of the main blower. With two hinged covers, 
all of the modules, components and fuses in the electric panel are eas ily accessible and quickly 
replaceable. The magnetics are positioned just behind the electrical panel.

Input Disconnect (Q1): 
The input disconnect/breaker disconnects all power to the PCA. Upon closing the input disconnect, the 
PCA passes through an initialisation test and then into standby mode.  
 
Input Contactor (Q2): 
The PCA is equipped with a 3 -phase input contactor. The contactor is engaged on start-up of the PCA 
and is disengaged when the stop button is activated or a system error occurs.  
 
Step-up Transformer, Input Choke and 12-Pulse Transformer (T5, L1 and T1): 
To accommodate the compressors ’ extended frequency range, the voltage is stepped up by T5. The 
following combination of the choke, the 12-pulse transformer and the rectifier situated at the DC module 
ensures an almost sinusoidal line current with a Total Current Harmonic Distortion of just 10% (ITHD) at 
nominal load. This means less stress on the main supply network and distribution transformers. 
 
 
DC Module (A9), Softstart and Busbar: 
Through the 12 -pulse rectifier (V1 -V6) and soft -start circuit 
(Q8), the DC is distributed to all of the VFDs, from a common 
DC busbar. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Supply Module (G1): 
The generation of the 24 VDC (adjusted from factory = 25 VDC) regulated control voltage is done by 
the Supply Module G1. It is pre-fused from the 3-phase circuit breaker Q9.

3.3 Control Boards and Display Boards 
The Control Board (A1) is based on a MicroController and a DSP. Together they regulate, supervise and 
diagnose possible external and internal faults. As soon as the PCA is connected to the mains, and 
constantly during normal operation, the Control Board runs a self -check program which checks all 
internal functions of the ground power unit. If an internal or external error is detected, the display shows 
the nature of the error. All immediate parameters related to a shutdown are stored in the PCA’s memory. 
The Control Board has an on-board Ethernet RJ45 connector, which can be used to communicate with 
the BMS and USB host Type A connection to retrieve data from the PCA or to update the software. 
 
The unit may be equipped with standard or alternative types of Control Boards and Display Boards. 
These boards can be used in any combination.

3.4 Modular cooling design   
Depending on the configuration and rating, the ITW GSE 3500 
PCA is equipped with 2-4 cooling modules. A module can easily 
be pulled out of the ITW GSE 3500 PCA by means of a forklif t 
and replaced by a new module.  
 
 
As this operation does not cause any interruption to the cooling 
circuit of a given module, it can be done by staff without any 
specific knowledge of refrigeration. 
 
 
 
The modular design of the ITW GSE 3500 PCA ensures that it is able to continue to provide cooling even 
when one or more of its modules are inactive. This does of course reduce the overall capacity of the 
PCA, but the reduced cooling may still be sufficient to prevent the aircraft’s APU from starting up. The 
cooling circuit is designed to provide high efficiency , as well as good rigidity of the unit. In addition, the 
cooling modules come with hermetically closed scroll compressors, which are specially designed for 
variable frequency control. 
 
VFDs allow the unit to maximise performance during aircraft pull-down. Furthermore, the VFDs help the 
ITW GSE 3500 PCA to minimise energy use for maintenance cooling. 
 
Rigid all-aluminium micro-channel condenser coils ensure good heat dissipation. An expansion valve 
and a large evaporator face area ensure efficient cooling of the air supply, while multiple step cooling 
efficiently condenses the air humidity and protects the last cooling step from icing.

4.0 Transportation and Installation 
4.1 Storage before installation 
To ensure optimal storage conditions prior to mounting and installation, it is recommended that the ITW 
GSE 3500 PCA is stored inside to protect it from rain and excessive humidity when switched off. Only 
equipment wrapped in seaworthy packing should be stored outside. For storage conditions, please 
refer to section 5.0. 
 
In the event of long-term storage, the PCA should be unpacked and operated for a minimum of 2 
minutes every month, in Ventilation Mode / Narrow Body Aircraft, to keep the main blower bearings in 
good condition. 
 
 
Do not stand, walk, crawl or place heavy items on top of the PCA 
during handling, storage, installation and operation. Standing, 
walking, crawling or placing heavy items may damage the roof.  
 
4.2 Disposal of packaging material 
The ITW GSE 3500 PCA is shipped on a wooden pallet and covered with a tarpaulin and plastic. 
These materials can be recycled and must be disposed of in accordance with the applicable laws and 
regulations of the relevant country. All wood used in the packaging of the ITW GSE 3500 is in 
compliance with ISPM-15 and is not subjected to any chemical conservation treatment. 
 
4.3 Disposal of unit (end of life) 
All ITW GSE products are marked with the crossed-out wheelie bin 
symbol, and state the date of manufacture on the nameplates/rating 
plates in accordance with EU Directive 2012/19/EU on waste 
electrical and electronic equipment (WEEE).  
 
The Directive states that electrical and electronic equipment (EEE) on 
reaching the end of its life must not be disposed of as unsorted 
municipal/local waste. WEEE must be disposed of at appropriate 
collection points and not in the normal waste stream. WEEE covers 
all EEE used by consumers and EEE intended for professional use. 
 
The ITW GSE 3500 consists of metal (steel frame, ducts and plates), non-ferrous metals (copper and 
aluminium), refrigerant (R410A or R454B), electronic components (PCBs, capacitors, fuses, MCBs and 
contactors), wires and cables. Refer to Appendix 08, for information for disposal of refrigerant. 
 
Non-recyclable materials must be disposed of according to applicable environmental laws and 
regulations in each country. The waste and recycling service providers must be properly authorised by 
national environmental agencies to carry out these activities.

4.4 Operational and environmental conditioning after installation 
When the ITW GSE 3500 PCA has been installed, the unit should preferably be kept with the input 
power on at all times to provide optimal conditions for the compressors and electronic components and 
to avoid humidity in the form of condensate water from reaching vital parts. 
 
 
It is strongly recommended to allow the compressors to pre-heat 
for 24 hours after applying the input power and before starting 
the PCA. 
 
 
The PCA will automatically run a 3-hour pre-heating cycle, if the PCA has been without input voltage 
for more than 1 hour AND the ambient temperature + compressor discharge temperatures are below 
18°C / 64°F. During the pre-heating cycle, cooling modes are disabled and the below message is 
shown. Ventilation and heating modes are available during compressor pre-heating.

PCA, e.g. by means of a central computer. The port is

The protocol is limited to MODBUS TCP/IP. The data 
available from the PCA is comprehensive and should meet 
most requirements.  
 
Supported Modbus function codes 
 
The ITW GSE 3500 implements a subset of the Modbus 
Application Protocol Specification V.1.1b. 
 
The following function codes are supported in Modbus requests: 
- 03 (0x03) Read Holding Registers 
- 04 (0x04) Read Input Registers 
- 06 (0x06) Write Single Register 
- 16 (0x10) Write Multiple Registers 
 
Here is a summary of the different kinds of data available: 
• The most common modes are: system in use, system in standby and system in fault. 
 
• The PCA provides a comprehensive range of internal and external parameter measurements 
and information, e.g. voltages, currents, temperatures, time, date etc. 
 
• The PCA provides two kinds of logs:   
The Black Box, which contains the last 100 errors detected together with all relevant 
parameters and status information available in connection with a shutdown. 
The Power Log, which contains the time in use and the power consumption for the last 100 
operations.

7.3 Hose compensation 
The airflow through an air delivery system (e.g. aircraft service hose, hose retriever, transition, 
telescopic duct etc.) generates a pressure drop between the PCA and the aircraft. This pressure drop 
will be added to the back pressure in the aircraft, resulting in a total outlet pressure (air delivery system 
+ aircraft back pressure = total outlet pressure), which is what the PCA unit will experience. 
 
If the PCA unit reaches maximum pressure (limits are shown in section 7.1) before full airflow is 
reached, then this may be due to the pressure drop in the air delivery system. The PCA unit can 
compensate for the pressure drop in the air delivery system by adding a Hose Compensation to the 
maximum pressure setting. The PCA unit will thus be able to increase to the requested airflow and 
provide a better performance.  
 
Note: Even with Hose Compensation set to maximum (4000 Pa / 16 inH2O), the outlet pressure can 
never exceed the maximum rating for the unit.

7.5 Intelligent Power Management (IPM) 
With an Intelligent Power Management-enabled PCA, it is possible to simplify the complete installation, 
achieve savings on infrastructure and use less space in the electrical room. 
The GPU(s) input cable is connected to a circuit breaker in the PCA’s electrical panel instead of the 
electrical room. (See figure 7.5.1 for an installation example.) For further details about IPM-related 
hardware, see section 11. 
 
 
Figure 7.5.1 Code C installation example 
 
The PCA will monitor the total power consumption of the PCA and GPU(s). If the total current exceeds 
a pre-set limit, the GPU(s) will be given priority to continue operation, whereas the PCA will adjust its 
own consumption to keep the total current below the limit. In the event of a 400Hz peak load, the result 
is a minor drop in the PCA’s cooling for a short moment, until there is available power again for the 
PCA to regulate back to normal operation. Figure 7.5.2 shows an example of a code C load profile 
during a turnaround. At 17.00h-17.02h, the 400Hz load is peaking, which results in a reduction of the 
PCA cooling. After the 400Hz peak, at 17.02h, the PCA automatically regulates back up. 
 
 
Figure 7.5.2 Example of Code C aircraft load profile

8.2 Battery back-up & replacement (only applicable for Coin Cell) 
 
Note: The following section is only applicable for the Coin Cell battery type. The on-soldered 
battery type, on the alternative Control Board 579573, does not need to be changed in the 
expected lifetime of the unit. 
 
Situated on the back of the control board, is a coin type lithium battery which ensures that firmware, 
setup data etc. are not lost during mains/utility drop-outs. The expected life of the battery is 
approximately 7 years. A low battery voltage does not affect the internal safety system of the PCA that 
monitors the output voltage, among others. Thus aircraft connected to the PCA are not exposed to any 
danger. However, to avoid loss of data and Control Board lock-up *, we require the battery to be 
changed every 5 years.

9.3 Smoke detector 
The PCA is equipped with an optical type smoke detector, which monitors the air that is delivered into 
the aircraft. 
 
The status of the smoke detector can be read via the LED located on the front 
lower part of the smoke detector, marked with the blue circle in fig. 9.3.1: 
 
Standby Mode:  Green LED flashes every 5 sec. 
Alarm Mode:  Red LED constantly lit. 
 
Smoke detector reset procedure 
If smoke is detected, the smoke detector will pass into Alarm Mode and 
activate an alarm relay signal to the control module. The PCA will immediately 
shut down and show an error on the display.  
 
In the event of an alarm, please use one of the following methods: 
 
Recommended method: The smoke detector can be reset by briefly 
disconnecting the input power to the PCA. 
 
Alternative method: The smoke detector can be reset by pressing and 
releasing the Test/Reset push button, marked with a red circle in fig. 9.3.1 or 
briefly short-circuiting wire terminals X12:7 and X12:8.   Thereafter, the PCA 
can be reset on the PCA remote control panel.                                  
 
 
Figure 9.3.1: Smoke detector 
 
 
• When inspecting/testing the smoke detector, keep away from electrical parts. 
• Touching live electrical parts can cause fatal shocks and severe burns. 
• Only allow qualified people to remove covers for maintenance or troubleshooting. 
 
Testing the smoke detector 
The smoke detector can be tested by pressing and holding (for at least 2 seconds) the Test/Reset

RCM troubleshooting guidance 
The RCM system consists of two components:  
• Current transformer, T7, measuring on the DC module input cables  
• RCM relay, A5, situated below the control section and connected to the interface board, A2, on 
terminals X6, 9-10 (see separate diagram for further details) 
 
If the PCA is in standby mode and there is a persistent error, it is likely that there is a problem with the 
wiring, relay settings, relay and/or control module. It would therefore be relevant to check the relay 
settings, wiring, relay and/or control section. 
 
If the PCA trips with an error during operation, it is likely due to a residual current in either a cooling 
module or in the heater section. 
 
To check a cooling module, disconnect the compressor cables, U-V-W, on the VFD and test the 
compressor with a megohmmeter between the phases and ground (max. voltage: 1000 VDC). A 
correct reading should be >10 MΩ.  
 
Note: Observe correct phasing during re-connection of the compressor 
 
To check the heater section, disconnect the “-” input cable from the common minus busbar, marked 
with a red circle below on the heater section. Test the heater section with a megohmmeter between the 
“-“ cables on the secondary side of common minus busbar (marked with blue circle) and ground (max. 
voltage: 1000 VDC). A correct reading should be >10 MΩ. Disconnect individual cables and repeat, if 
further troubleshooting is needed. Re-connect all cables after completed troubleshooting.

572 840 Intelligent Power Management – PCA 150 + 1 GPU 
With an Intelligent Power Management-enabled PCA, it is possible to simplify the complete installation, 
achieve savings on infrastructure and use less space in the electrical room. 
The GPU(s) input cable is connected to a circuit breaker in the PCA’s electrical panel instead of the 
electrical room, and the PCA will then monitor the total power consumption of the PCA and GPU(s). If 
the total current exceeds a pre-set limit, the GPU(s) will be given priority to continue operation, 
whereas the PCA will adjust its own consumption to keep the total current below the limit. In the event 
of a 400 Hz peak load, the result is a minor drop in the PCA’s cooling for a short moment until there is 
available power again for the PCA to regulate back to normal operation. 
 
The option includes: 
• One 3-phase 150A circuit breaker for a GPU 
• Transparent safety cover inside the electrical panel 
• Integrated current monitoring capability and fully automatic SW-based PCA capacity regulation 
 
Not included: 
• Power cables to the PCA and between the PCA and GPU 
 
Please note that the maximum overcurrent protection is 315 A. Consult your ITW GSE sales contact 
for further advice regarding PCA and GPU consumption.
